Overview

Crossword League reimagines a solitary format as a live, social competition, designed around real-time multiplayer flows for up to a dozen players across five distinct game modes. Every screen balances a fast-moving game board against chat, scoring, and social overlays, with interactive prototypes built to test live usability and game-state feedback under real network conditions.

Key Highlights
  • Onboarding & matchmaking: Designed the lobby and mode-selection flow to get players from landing page to first move in seconds, cutting the friction points (mode selection, lobby setup, ready-checks) that typically stall multiplayer games before they start.
  • Screen real estate under pressure: Solved the core layout problem of live multiplayer — keeping the crossword grid legible while surfacing chat, opponent cursors, and score state without crowding the primary task.
  • AI-assisted rapid prototyping: Used AI-assisted development to move past static comps and ship a fully working product concept — a design validation approach that let the interaction design (corruption meters, elimination countdowns, live multiplayer cursors) get pressure-tested by real players under real conditions, not just clicked through in a prototyping tool.
Problem

Crossword solving is built as a solitary experience, so there was no existing product pattern to design against. The core problem was translating a single-player interaction model into a shared, real-time space without breaking the feel of actually solving a puzzle — deciding whose progress counts when two players land on the same word at once, how that's shown fairly, and how the board stays readable when several people are acting on it simultaneously.

Game Modes
Play Together

Collaborative solving; all players share a live grid.

Versus

1v1 competitive scoring weighted by speed and accuracy.

Ranked Elimination

Players must solve a minimum number of clues each round or get eliminated. Survivors advance; the last player standing wins. A "carry-over" mechanic punishes consistent underperformance across rounds.

Battle Royale

Solving words charges an attack meter. Attacks corrupt opponents' grids (cracking or shattering cells). Players manage a corruption meter and survive through a grace period mechanic. Supports team variants (Red vs. Blue).

Race the Clock

Time-limited solo solve tracked against other players.

Design-to-Code Feasibility
Instant feedback, not staleness

Every player's fills, cursor, and score update on everyone else's screen the moment they happen. That immediacy was the whole design bet of this project — a shared board only feels "live" if nothing about it ever feels delayed or out of sync.

Designing fairness, not just logic

Ranked mode's elimination countdown includes an "all-safe" state that holds off the timer once every remaining player has already met the threshold — a small interaction decision that exists purely so eliminations never feel arbitrary or unfair to the player on the receiving end.

Making an abstract meter readable

Battle mode's corruption meter needed to communicate danger at a glance mid-game, so it's shown as cracking, shattering cells rather than a numeric readout — extending the same glanceable-hierarchy thinking from the UI into the gameplay itself.

Accessibility as a core mode, not an add-on

Six accessible modes were designed in from the start rather than retrofitted: high contrast, colorblind mode, screen reader support, adjustable font scaling, dyslexia-friendly typefaces, and fully remappable controls.

All of it was validated the same way the rest of the product was: use AI-assisted development to get a real, working version in front of real players fast, then iterate on what the interaction data and player feedback actually showed.

Engagement
XP and leveling

Earned through puzzle completions, wins, and chat activity.

Achievements

13+ unlock conditions (win streaks, chat volume, Z-word guesses, ranked survival, etc.) with animated unlock popups.

Weekly Challenges

Three rotating challenges selected per calendar week via a deterministic seed, tracked against a baseline snapshot taken at week start.

Friend system

Friend requests, a friends list, and in-game direct messaging, all backed by Firebase.

Pixel-art avatar creator

A client-side canvas drawing tool for personalizing player icons.

Outcome

Designed, shipped, and launched a live multiplayer game platform that reached thousands of pageviews and converted early visitors into 20+ registered active players. The final product delivers five distinct competitive modes, a full progression and social layer, and accessibility support across six dimensions — validated with real players, not just usability tests in a vacuum.
